# Anti-slop review

Look for unmade decisions, not a forbidden aesthetic. A deliberate gradient in an established brand is different from a generic glow used to fill space. Keep useful detail: removing ornaments without adding a stronger composition can leave an empty, unhelpful result.

## Diagnose and replace

| Symptom | Ask | Useful correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every section is a row of equal cards | Does the content really have equal roles? | Use the comparison, sequence, collection or demonstration the content requires |
| Hero could advertise any competitor | What specific behavior, place, object or proof belongs here? | Make that the central visual and headline |
| Generic claims dominate the page or narration | What can the audience see or verify? | Replace a broad promise with an observed action or sourced fact |
| Constant movement with no focus | Where should the eye be at this instant? | Let support elements rest; move only the cause, action or result |
| Repeated entrance on every scene | Has the information changed or just the decoration? | Vary framing and use cuts/holds where they communicate better |
| Fake interface or invented statistics | Is this an actual product state or clearly marked demonstration? | Use real UI/data or label the illustrative construction |
| Tiny annotation and crowded captions | Can someone read this at the final viewing size? | Shorten, recompose and extend the hold |
| Decorative badges, icons or labels | Does removing it remove information? | Keep functional labels; remove only redundant ornament |
| Uniform spacing despite different content | Which groups belong together and which need separation? | Adjust rhythm through existing tokens and semantic grouping |

## Review in priority order

1. Truth and purpose: recognizable subject, supported claims, useful content.
2. Hierarchy and readability: one primary focus, clear grouping, legible words.
3. Rhythm: enough variation to guide attention, enough repetition to feel coherent.
4. Craft: crops, alignment, type details, motion easing, audio edits and loop seams.

Name the three most consequential visible problems, fix them and inspect again. Stop when the brief is met and remaining changes are preference tradeoffs; do not endlessly chase a numerical taste score. Tool findings are evidence for review, not an automatic command to strip the brand or a substitute for looking.
