# Brief and art direction

## Resolve five decisions

Read the conversation and project first. Ask only questions whose answers materially change the output; present the missing ones together with useful options. An existing answer is still an answer, even if it was informal.

| Decision | Useful question | Examples of choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose and audience | Who is this for, and what should they understand or do? | Explain a mechanism; request a demo; buy a named offer |
| Deliverable and context | Where will this be seen, and what must be delivered? | 20-second vertical film; responsive landing page; UI loop inside an existing hero |
| Direction and fidelity | What should this feel like, and which existing identity must survive? | Editorial and quiet; bold typography; faithful supplied UI |
| Content and proof | Which assets, claims and references are real and approved for use? | Product screenshots; supplied footage; verified offer; no existing imagery |
| Motion, audio and constraints | What moves or speaks, and what limits matter? | Polish narration and captions; silent loop; reduced motion; local tools; approved budget |

Do not ask for all possible formats, tools and preferences. For a small known task, one question or none may suffice. If the user cannot supply a reversible design decision, propose a stated assumption. Missing essential source material or a requested approval remains unresolved until answered.

For Social Motion Agent films, save these five categories in `intake.json` using actual user-supplied answers and their context. Preserve the existing record structure and explicit waiver support. Do not invent a conversation to satisfy validation. This film record requirement is not a requirement to ask five new questions every time.

## Show what a direction changes

Each concept should state:

- **Idea:** a feature of this subject that becomes a visual device, not just a category aesthetic.
- **Placement:** where the idea appears, such as the hero, comparison section, opening shot or transition into proof.
- **Composition:** focal object, type hierarchy, image treatment and density.
- **Motion:** one clear behavior and its purpose, with a quiet/static fallback for web.
- **Requirements:** assets that exist, assets still needed, and any actual runtime dependency.

Example for a real scheduling product:

| Direction | Where and what | Motion | Needed proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Working calendar | Hero shows the real weekly view; next section isolates a collision | A requested slot moves into a genuinely available gap | Approved UI and a representative booking flow |
| Editorial time study | Oversized time labels beside a narrated sequence of user actions | Cuts follow each completed task; numbers remain fixed unless sourced | Measured timings, or omit numerical savings |
| Quiet product close-up | Two deliberate crops show the input and confirmed result | Cursor action, state change, short reading hold | Actual recording or a clearly labelled reconstruction |

Do not make three palette swaps and call them three concepts. Vary the information structure or visual mechanism. A styleboard can show a hero at desktop and mobile plus one film frame; keep demo claims labelled and avoid building the entire product before the direction is chosen.

## Lock decisions lightly

Save a concise direction note in the task's existing working files. Include the chosen idea, asset inventory, type/color roles, motion character and what must not change. On an existing project, write how the direction extends its current system. Revisit the note after feedback; do not introduce a second competing visual language halfway through production.

A useful self-check: replace the subject with its closest competitor. If every word and visual still fits unchanged, find a more specific product behavior, material, place, shape or proof to carry the work.
